Earth + Fire: how magma pathways form

An interactive conceptual walkthrough of partial melting, buoyancy, and fractures in rock.

From hot rock to a pathway

When mantle rock gets hot enough, some minerals melt. Melt can collect in pores and fractures. Because magma is often less dense than surrounding solid rock, buoyancy can help it rise. If pressure opens cracks, magma may travel through dikes and feed a volcanic system.

The actual temperature and melt composition vary with pressure, water content, and rock type. The controls below are visual teaching aids, not a predictive volcano model.

Moderate
12%

Partial melt gathers along grain boundaries, where small connected films can begin to move.

  1. Heat and decompressionTemperature rises, pressure falls, or volatiles lower the melting point.
  2. Partial meltingSome minerals melt while others remain solid; melt composition changes as it separates.
  3. Melt segregationInterconnected melt moves through pores, grain boundaries, and fractures.
  4. Dike and conduit growthPressurized magma can open fractures; repeated movement may establish pathways.

What do gluons do in the Standard Model?

Reading and audio

Magma forms when some rock melts. Melt can gather, rise, and move through cracks. Quarks are bound by the strong interaction carried by gluons. The scale models on this page are illustrative.
